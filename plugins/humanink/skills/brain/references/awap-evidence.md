# AWAP evidence — your research is proof of authorship

This is what HumanInk Brain has that a plain second brain does not. A writer who
builds a world, researches it, and queries their own canon is *doing the human
work of authorship*. The brain records that work as evidence.

## What gets logged

When `config.yaml: awap.log_events` is true and the AWAP tools are available
(installed with HumanInk Everything or Certificate), log these as writing events:

- **Capture** — the writer drops material into the brain (their own notes, research).
- **Build** — raw is organized into wiki articles (curation is authorship).
- **Ask / audit** — the writer interrogates and reconciles their own canon.
- **Import / continuity** — backfilling and cross-checking a manuscript.

Each event records what kind of work it was, the scope (which book), and when —
never the full content unless the writer wants it. Use the AWAP log tool the same
way the Auditor (17) does.

## Why it strengthens the score

The Human Authorship Score rewards *process evidence*: that a human conceived,
researched, organized and decided. A rich, time-stamped brain trail is exactly the
kind of evidence the U.S. Copyright Office looks for in AI-assisted work. The brain
turns the writer's everyday note-keeping into a defensible record — quietly, in the
background.

## Rules

- **Opt-in and quiet.** Logging is on by default but never interrupts; the writer
  can turn it off in `config.yaml`.
- **Local-first.** Events live with the writer's AWAP record; nothing is published
  without the writer signing a certificate (the Auditor's job).
- **Honest.** Log what actually happened — captured/organized/queried — never inflate.

If the brain is installed standalone (no AWAP tools present), skip logging silently
and work as a pure second brain.
