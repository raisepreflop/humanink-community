# Continuity audit

Surface contradictions *inside the writer's canon*, scoped to one book or series.
You report; the writer decides. Never "fix" canon silently.

## What to check (within scope only)

- **Entity attributes** — a trait stated two ways (eyes green / eyes brown).
- **Timeline** — events whose order or dates don't add up; ages that drift.
- **Status** — someone established as dead/absent who later acts; a destroyed
  object that reappears.
- **Relationships** — a stated relationship contradicted later.
- **World rules** — a rule of the world broken without in-story cause.

## How

1. Scope to one book or series (never-pool). Read the wiki + the relevant raw.
2. For each candidate contradiction, gather **both** supporting passages with
   their provenance (article + which book + line).
3. Present a short table: *claim A (where) ⟷ claim B (where) — what conflicts.*
4. Rank by how load-bearing the fact is, not by how many hits.
5. Offer options — the writer chooses which is canon — then, only on their
   say-so, update the wiki/raw to match.

## Rules

- **Evidence both sides.** Never assert a contradiction without quoting both passages.
- **In-scope only.** A "contradiction" across two different books in different
  series is not a contradiction — it's two worlds. Don't flag it.
- **Silence is fine.** "No contradictions found in <scope>; here's what I
  checked" is a complete, valuable answer.

This pairs with the Desk Editor (06) and the Auditor (17): a clean continuity
pass is part of a defensible, well-kept manuscript. Log it as an authorship event.
