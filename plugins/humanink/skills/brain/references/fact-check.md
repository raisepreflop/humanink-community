# Fact-check (real-world truth)

Canon questions are answered from the brain (`ask.md`). *Real-world* questions —
"would a 1890s revolver have a safety?", "is this Latin correct?", "how long does
rigor mortis take?" — are answered against reality, not the writer's files. Keep
the two streams separate so neither pollutes the other.

## Verdicts

Give a clear verdict, then the basis:

- **Confirmed** — well-established; state it plainly.
- **Likely** — supported but with caveats; name them.
- **Disputed / depends** — sources disagree or it's conditional; lay out the conditions.
- **Unsupported** — you can't substantiate it; say so rather than guess.

## Rules

- **Separate from canon.** A fact-check result is not added to the book's canon
  wiki unless the writer asks; if saved, it goes to `reference/` (whole-writer) or
  the book's `outputs/`, labelled as research, with its date.
- **Cite the basis** in plain terms; flag where current web research would help
  (use it if available) versus where it's settled knowledge.
- **No false confidence.** For anything safety-, legal- or medically-sensitive,
  state the uncertainty and that it's craft research, not professional advice.
- **Period accuracy** is a common ask: distinguish "anachronistic" from "rare but
  possible" and let the writer choose.

Saving a fact-check to `reference/` lets it be reused across books — that's the
point of the whole-writer level.
